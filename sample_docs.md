## 1. What is a semiconductor
A semiconductor is a material with electrical conductivity between
that of a conductor (like copper) and an insulator (like glass).
Silicon is the most common semiconductor material used in the
electronics industry because of its abundance and stable properties
at normal operating temperatures.

## 2. Doping
Doping is the process of intentionally introducing impurities into
a pure semiconductor to change its electrical properties. Adding
elements like phosphorus creates an n-type semiconductor with extra
free electrons, while adding elements like boron creates a p-type
semiconductor with extra "holes" (missing electrons).

## 3. The P-N junction
A P-N junction is formed when p-type and n-type semiconductor
materials are joined together. This junction is the basic building
block of diodes and transistors, allowing current to flow easily in
one direction while blocking it in the other.

## 4. Transistors
A transistor is a semiconductor device used to amplify or switch
electronic signals. The invention of the transistor in 1947 at Bell
Labs replaced bulky vacuum tubes and enabled the miniaturization of
electronic circuits, leading directly to modern integrated circuits.

## 5. Moore's Law
Moore's Law is the observation made by Gordon Moore in 1965 that the
number of transistors on a microchip roughly doubles every two years,
while the cost of computing power decreases. This trend drove decades
of rapid progress in semiconductor manufacturing, though physical
limits have begun to slow it in recent years.

## 6. Integrated circuits
An integrated circuit (IC), or microchip, is a set of electronic
circuits built on a small flat piece of semiconductor material,
usually silicon. Modern ICs can contain billions of transistors on
a chip smaller than a fingernail, combining logic, memory, and other
functions on a single piece of silicon.

## 7. Wafer fabrication
Semiconductor manufacturing starts with a thin, round slice of
silicon called a wafer. Wafers go through hundreds of processing
steps — including deposition, etching, and doping — inside a
cleanroom facility called a fab, to build up the layers of circuitry
that eventually become individual chips.

## 8. Photolithography
Photolithography is the process used to print circuit patterns onto
a silicon wafer. Light is shone through a patterned mask onto a
light-sensitive chemical coating, transferring the pattern to the
wafer. Modern chips use extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography to
create features only a few nanometers wide.

## 9. Band gap
The band gap is the energy difference between a material's valence
band (where electrons normally sit) and its conduction band (where
electrons can move freely and carry current). Semiconductors have a
small band gap, which is why their conductivity can be controlled —
unlike insulators, which have a large band gap, or conductors, which
have none.

## 10. Silicon vs. compound semiconductors
Silicon is the dominant semiconductor material due to its low cost
and abundance, but compound semiconductors like gallium arsenide
(GaAs) and gallium nitride (GaN) are used in specialized applications
such as high-frequency radio circuits, LEDs, and power electronics,
where they outperform silicon.

## 11. MOSFET
A MOSFET (Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor) is the
most common type of transistor used in modern digital electronics.
It acts as a switch controlled by voltage on its "gate" terminal,
and its small size and low power consumption make it the building
block of virtually all modern computer chips.

## 12. Diodes
A diode is one of the simplest semiconductor devices, built from a
single P-N junction. It allows electric current to flow in only one
direction, which makes it useful for tasks like converting
alternating current (AC) to direct current (DC), and for protecting
circuits from reversed voltage.

## 13. Semiconductor foundries
A foundry is a company that manufactures chips designed by other
companies, rather than designing its own. TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor
Manufacturing Company) is the world's largest foundry, producing
chips for companies like Apple and Nvidia who design the chips but
do not operate their own fabrication plants.

## 14. Memory chips: DRAM and NAND flash
Two major categories of semiconductor memory are DRAM (Dynamic
Random-Access Memory), used for a computer's fast working memory but
which loses data when powered off, and NAND flash, used in SSDs and
USB drives, which retains data without power. Both rely on
transistor and capacitor structures built using semiconductor
fabrication techniques.

## 15. Heat and semiconductor performance
Semiconductor devices generate heat as current flows through them,
and this heat can degrade performance or damage the device if not
managed. As transistors have shrunk and been packed more densely
per Moore's Law, heat dissipation has become one of the major
engineering challenges limiting how fast and how densely chips can
be built.
